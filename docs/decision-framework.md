# Decision framework

## Node comparison and optional unit eligibility

1. Screen every planning area with compatible data, recording missingness and proxy quality.
2. Define commercial nodes with documented selection rules. Preserve screened-out areas and reasons. Use multiple screening signals and coverage review so a single proxy does not eliminate promising areas without explanation.
3. Construct transparent node-level financial scenarios using a generic `operator_assumption` monthly occupancy-cost grid. State every value and test sensitivity. S016 may be consulted manually as restricted context but cannot be reproduced, stored or used to create published node-specific rent ranges.
4. Compare nodes using demand, accessibility, competition and financial scenarios. A conditional node recommendation does not require a current premises record and must not imply availability, quoted rent, permitted use or lease readiness.
5. Optionally investigate premises within candidate nodes. Record current availability, quoted and all-in rent, intended tuition use, public evidence, unit-specific authoritative evidence, usable teaching space, practical access and operating constraints. Do not infer permission from an education neighbour or a listing category.
6. Assign each investigated premises exactly one status: `verified_eligible`, `screened_feasible`, `ineligible` or `unresolved`. `verified_eligible` requires unit-specific authoritative evidence confirming every mandatory **premises** requirement in this framework. `screened_feasible` means currently available public evidence reveals no known premises blocker, while final permission or landlord confirmation remains. `ineligible` means at least one confirmed mandatory premises failure. `unresolved` means the premises evidence is insufficient or contradictory.

Legal and operational readiness has three separate layers: (a) premises use and fire safety under the applicable URA/SCDF paths; (b) school/operator registration and related applicability under the Education Act/MOE process, informed by S024 and S025; and (c) ordinary commercial and lease due diligence such as title/authority, lease terms, costs, insurance and contractual risk. The premises-status enum covers layer (a) only. It does not certify layers (b) or (c), and a unit may not be called legally ready merely because URA/SCDF checks pass. Applicability and pass/fail remain fact- and unit-specific; this project records decision checks and is not legal, regulatory or professional advice.

## Hypotheses recorded before results

| ID | Testable expectation | Planned comparison and possible challenge |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Accessible resident target-age population is positively associated with confirmed in-scope branch presence/count. | Test using the frozen catchment and branch definitions, with sensitivity to age allocation, catchment specification, uncertain coverage, confounding, small samples and spatial dependence. This is observational; existing placement is revealed location behaviour, not proof of success or causality. |
| H2 | Transit-aware accessibility differs materially from straight-line accessibility. | Compare accessible population and node ordering under frozen transit-aware and straight-line methods. Define the numerical materiality rule before final results are inspected. If the OneMap preflight fails, report the honest proximity fallback and do not claim this hypothesis was tested with transit times. |

These are the only active pre-results hypotheses. Record H2's numerical definition of “materially different” before final results are inspected. The former population-only versus full-shortlist hypothesis is now a descriptive decision contrast with no hypothesis-test threshold. The former 70% rule is now robustness reporting: publish non-dominated and top-three frequencies, denominators and rank distributions over the complete versioned scenario/profile grid, with no 70% pass/fail threshold and no probability interpretation. Education-cluster effects and expansion cannibalisation are optional analyses, not core hypotheses.

## Pareto comparison

Apply data-quality and minimum-feasibility gates first. Within each complete operating scenario, compare only admissible commercial nodes on four dimensions. An alternative dominates another when it is at least as good on every dimension and strictly better on at least one. Publish definitions, directions, tolerances, the full comparison set and the generic rent assumptions. A premises status is not required for node-level comparison. If optional unit alternatives are later compared, do so separately and preserve their premises statuses. Do not treat missing values as zero, best, worst or a passing result; incomplete alternatives get `insufficient_data`.

| Criterion | Direction | Planned measurement |
|---|---|---|
| Local demand proxy | Maximise | Area-weighted resident ages 7–16 population proxy within the 800 m union of preserved station exits; it is not observed demand or customers |
| Transit accessibility | Maximise | Resident ages 7–16 population proxy reachable within each documented transit-time threshold; this wider network reach is kept separate from the local 800 m proxy |
| Competition | Minimise | Confirmed in-scope branch count in the catchment; where possible counts are complete, report `confirmed + all possible`; where they are not, state that gap and use the frozen zero-confirmed caution stress test rather than inventing an upper bound |
| Financial viability | Maximise | One dimension formed by averaging within-dimension percentile ranks of the selected, direction-aligned financial metrics (for example lower break-even utilisation and higher maximum affordable monthly occupancy cost) |

Show confirmed and possible branch counts, coverage and quality flags. The national run did not retain possible leads consistently across every batch, so those values remain null and the release adds a labelled zero-confirmed caution case: a zero-confirmed node is scored as one competition unit solely to test whether its result depends on the automatic best score. This does not claim that an unobserved branch exists. Distance bands and level overlap are descriptive; no fractional branch weights are used in the primary measure. Decay/overlap formulations may be secondary sensitivity only and cannot replace the primary criterion after results are seen. Branches per 1,000 target residents remains descriptive because target population is already a separate dimension. A zero or unknown denominator yields null plus a reason. `required_capture_share` also divides by the population proxy, so it is interpretive and must not be included again in the financial dimension. The local 800 m demand proxy and wider network accessibility proxy answer different questions and are reported separately. Education-cluster measures remain separate and optional.

Pareto membership is not an overall ranking and can include many alternatives. Generic financial assumptions shared by every node create ties; unsupported node-specific rent differences may not be introduced to make the financial dimension discriminate. For every complete operating scenario, calculate the frontier, then rank admissible non-dominated nodes under exactly five dimension-weight profiles: balanced `25/25/25/25`; demand `40/20/20/20`; accessibility `20/40/20/20`; competition `20/20/40/20`; and finance `20/20/20/40`, in demand/accessibility/competition/finance order. Rank direction-aligned percentile scores; if finance uses multiple metrics, average their percentile ranks inside the finance dimension before applying its profile weight so finance is not accidentally double-weighted.

Across every scenario × profile combination, calculate top-three frequency. A release recommendation must appear at least once under both observed-confirmed and zero-confirmed-caution cases; this prevents an area from qualifying solely because no competitor was confirmed. Among eligible nodes, select up to three by highest total top-three frequency. Break ties by lower median rank, then lower worst-case rank, then stable node ID. Report selection counts, denominators, frequencies and full rank distributions for every admissible candidate. Do not force three when fewer pass gates. This is a transparent preference sensitivity procedure, not proof of an objectively best node.

## Recommendation format

Each shortlisted node must show its catchment and age-proxy method; local and travel-time population proxies; confirmed competition; possible counts/upper bound where valid or explicit null status and the caution stress-test result where unavailable; financial assumptions and the generic `operator_assumption` rent grid; break-even active enrolments, break-even utilisation, `required_capture_share`, `maximum_affordable_monthly_occupancy_cost`, adverse cases and rent sensitivity; scenario-specific Pareto status; top-three frequencies and rank distributions; unresolved risks; and a next due-diligence action. Label the recommendation conditional and do not imply that a current unit is available, quoted, permitted, registered or lease-ready. If optional premises evidence is included, show its premises status, public/unit-specific evidence and remaining premises confirmations, plus separate school/operator-registration and commercial/lease checks. `verified_eligible` is premises-specific and must not be presented as universal legal readiness. Do not imply simultaneous openings are optimal: joint expansion needs overlap-adjusted demand and optional network analysis.
