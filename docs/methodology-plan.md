Foundational run `foundation_7dbd9a143e9504e7` fully processed the preferred 2025 single-year-age population, MP2019 boundaries, 2026 schools, MRT exits and bus stops. A reproducible current premises/rent feed and unit-specific eligibility evidence are not available; this blocks current-unit and lease-ready recommendations, but not conditional node comparison using transparent rent and operating scenarios.

Before final results are inspected, define numerical materiality for H2, define the complete plausible operating-scenario grid, choose stakeholder travel thresholds, and freeze node-selection and primary catchment rules. Log later deviations from the two pre-results hypotheses. The population-only versus full-decision shortlist remains a descriptive contrast, while non-dominated and top-three frequencies are robustness reports without a 70% pass/fail threshold. Dynamic branch, offering, fee and premises evidence expires after 30 days; regulatory guidance is rechecked within 90 days and unit evidence within 30 days of a decision.

## 2. Acquire and harmonise later

Preserve acquired snapshots without in-place editing; record source/retrieval dates, byte size, SHA-256, extraction parameters, observed schema, row count and transformation versions. Keep raw observations separate from resolved entities. Produce compatible planning-area and, where supported, subzone tables. Treat boundary changes as explicit crosswalks; validate population conservation and flag approximate allocation.

Use GeoJSON/WGS84 (EPSG:4326, longitude/latitude) for exchange and **SVY21 / Singapore TM (EPSG:3414)** for distance and area calculations, supported by SLA's SVY21 and OneMap coordinate documentation. Record the transformation implementation and round-trip precision. Never calculate metres from degrees.

## 3. Define the target-population proxy

The intended interval is resident ages 7–16 inclusive. Preferred source S001 publishes June 2025 residents by MP2019 planning-area and subzone names, single year of age and sex. The bounded inspection found no stable geography IDs and left full duplicate-key/name-join validation for later ingestion. When national transformation is separately authorised, sum total-sex ages 7 through 16 directly, preserve single-year rows and reconcile planning-area totals to subzones. Age still remains a proxy for P1–S4 rather than an exact school-level measure. Secondary 5 is outside scope although age 16 can overlap S5, and Primary 1 balloting is not a core input.

If S001 fails preflight, use S002 Census 2020 grouped ages 5–9, 10–14 and 15–19 with the explicitly stale, uniform-within-band approximation:

`target_proxy = (3/5 × residents_5_9) + residents_10_14 + (2/5 × residents_15_19)`.

The fractions select ages 7–9 and 15–16; they are allocation assumptions, not observed age distributions. Preserve the original groups. A conservative identification interval with only these groups is `[residents_10_14, residents_5_9 + residents_10_14 + residents_15_19]`; it is broad by design, not a confidence interval. Later use supported age profiles for tighter scenarios if available, without claiming local precision from national profiles. Recompute using ages 6–15 and 8–17 where feasible to test cohort alignment. For grouped inputs these shifted ranges can yield identical estimates under uniform allocation; report that lack of discriminatory information.

Allocate to catchments using the smallest reliable source geography. Area-weighted allocation assumes uniform residential density and must be labelled accordingly; residential-weighted allocation requires independently supported residential weights. Preserve population totals within tolerance. Avoid distributing residents over industrial/open land without acknowledging the approximation. Do not sum overlapping catchment populations into a national or multi-centre total.

## 4. Screen nationally, then define nodes and access

Produce a coverage-aware planning-area comparison of age-proxy demand, branch presence, transport context and premises-market evidence. Retain the full national comparison and exclusion log. Agree a manageable candidate set only after coverage review; do not set a numerical top-k cut-off before observing inventory coverage.

Use this node rule before final results:

1. Form a rail-station complex from S005 exits sharing the same station/complex identity; keep all exits and use their medoid in EPSG:3414 as the access anchor. Manually adjudicate interchange naming/merges.
2. Permit a non-rail node only when it is a named HDB/URA town or neighbourhood centre supported by a directly opened official page and a reproducible anchor.
3. Qualify either node type only when at least two independent current commercial signals are documented, drawn from active in-scope branches, permitted premises evidence, an official named commercial centre, or usable URA market evidence. Transport points alone do not make a commercial node.
4. Preserve rejected/unqualified candidates and reasons. Review 100% of shortlisted node definitions. Nodes may cross planning-area boundaries.

A bare alphanumeric S005 station label (a line/interchange code with no full station name) is never merged or renamed by exit proximity alone. It is resolved only through a versioned `station_overrides` registry entry backed by a dated, authoritative LTA source (an official code-to-station mapping or an official opening announcement); until such an entry exists it stays a separate, flagged, `unresolved`-operational-status complex. Every complex carries an explicit `operational_status` of `verified_operational`, `verified_opening_within_decision_horizon`, `not_operational` or `unresolved`; a station confirmed to open only after the frozen analysis cutoff is excluded from current accessibility and commercial-node candidates until a separately approved forward-looking scenario extends the decision horizon. Node review issues are independent and additive: a node with, for example, both an ambiguous identity and no commercial-zoning signal receives both queue items, deduplicated only by `(station_complex_id, issue_category)`.

A fully named MRT/LRT complex is never treated as `verified_operational` merely because it appears in the S005 exit layer: S005 can and does include stations that have not opened (for example Marina South, which LTA's own Thomson-East Coast Line page states will open only once nearby development completes, with the date to be advised). Exact-name corroboration by S026 (LTA, Jun 2017) is likewise never sufficient by itself: S026 can carry a name/code for a station that was not yet operating when it was published (Teck Lee LRT Station is an exact S026 match but did not begin operating until 15 August 2024), so S026 is used only for identity, name, station-code and line corroboration, never for operational status. `operational_status` instead follows a versioned `config/nodes/operational_status_evidence.json` policy, in order: an identity-override's own dated station-specific evidence; an explicit `known_exceptions` entry (the only route to `not_operational` or `verified_opening_within_decision_horizon`); a match against `config/nodes/system_map_roster.json`, a versioned derived registry read from LTA's own dated System Map after directly checking its legend for an explicit under-construction marker; otherwise `unresolved`. A same-labelled repeated-exit pair is always retained with its distinct S005 identifiers and coordinates regardless of distance; a disclosed metre-based QA heuristic decides only whether a semantic-ambiguity flag needs to stay open for human attention, and is never itself treated as proof that two same-labelled records are, or are not, physically separate real-world exit structures.

Qualifying a node still requires at least two genuinely independent positive commercial-signal families; MP2025 zoning's 400 m and 800 m bands remain one family. A predeclared, non-selective second-independent-signal validation protocol (freeze an expanded shortlist before testing, validate every shortlisted node against one current legally reusable commercial-presence source that is not zoning and not a tuition competitor, recompute and repeat until the eligible shortlist is stable) is the approved route to `qualified`; it is documented ahead of the acquisition it would require and is not implemented merely to obtain a qualified result.

OneMap S007 is a `verified_limited` routing source. Develop walking and PT catchments with recorded origin, destinations, mode, journey date/time window, max-walk/transfer assumptions, threshold and per-route result status. Stakeholder travel thresholds remain an explicit decision input and are frozen before results. For each HTTP 404/missing PT route, retain the missing value and use OneMap walking-network time/distance as first fallback. If the required routing result remains unavailable, calculate only EPSG:3414 straight-line distance to the nearest MRT exit and bus stop plus stop counts within declared metre bands. Name it `proximity_proxy`; do not populate `transit_reach_share` or claim a travel-time catchment. Never impute a missing PT time. Document overlap and test anchor placement effects.

D54 implements the offline straight-line baseline in run `proximity_baseline_965a36240409d092`. For each of the 146 current MRT-area candidates it unions buffers around every preserved exit, rather than buffering only the station medoid, and allocates the ages 7–16 subzone proxy by the share of EPSG:3414 polygon area intersected. The primary descriptive band is 800 m and 1,200 m is sensitivity. This assumes population is uniform within each subzone; overlapping node catchments cannot be summed. The output remains `proximity_proxy` only and does not satisfy the national routing, H2 or final `accessible_target_population_proxy` requirements.

D57/D58 freeze 500 m as the primary OneMap `maxWalkDistance`, with 300 m strict and 1,000 m generous bounded comparisons. The corrected request identity includes the walking parameter and other request-defining fields so outcomes from different settings cannot collide in the resumable cache. Bounded run `walk_comparison_11ab30dda2b46b67` used the same deterministic 20 origin–destination pairs across four straight-line distance bands and made 60 PT plus three required walking-fallback requests. Each walking setting produced 19 successful PT routes, one missing PT route and four journeys within the 20-minute primary threshold; every pair's status and duration were identical across settings. This diagnostic supports retaining 500 m but is not representative national evidence. The national 96,944-request run remains unapproved.

## 5. Resolve and validate competitor branches

Maintain separate brands, physical branches and branch offerings. One branch is one operator's identifiable, operating teaching location at a specific address/unit at the analysis cut-off. The same branch offering several levels is counted once; different chain locations count separately. Independent operators get separate brand identities. Two operators at one building are not automatically duplicates. A relocation is a new physical branch record linked to the old one; only the operating site counts at a given cut-off. Retain aliases and source records for audit.

Discovery and validation are separate. Freeze the cutoff, scope and standard search templates first. Seed candidates from S010's location/MRT/provider and Primary/Secondary Mathematics categories, then run the same web/map query set for every planning area; log queries, dates, result/page counts and zero-result searches. Resolve brands, aliases and physical locations. S011 may corroborate legal identity/status but not a teaching branch.

Implemented at pilot scale on 2026-09-13 (`src/tuition_location_analytics/competitors/`, `config/competitors/`): `confirmed` requires fresh operator-controlled evidence for branch existence, the mathematics subject and at least one overlapping P1-S4 level independently; a directory result (S010), ACRA record (S011) or search snippet alone can only ever support `possible`. S010 remains a bounded manual discovery sample only and must not be systematically acquired or publicly redistributed. Under D43, its observation and candidate-validation ledgers are private ignored local evidence, not tracked configuration; outputs retaining it are likewise private. The frozen 10-area × 2-channel × 2-template protocol has exactly 40 generated query records, each reconciled to its observation IDs. Candidate-specific validation alone supplies canonical branch fields and validation coverage; brand-level access attempts are diagnostics only. Under D44, a missing unit is absence rather than affirmative disagreement only when the same brand and deterministic building-address key have one complete linked candidate-specific canonical address/postal/unit identity. Multiple known units, postal conflicts or explicit identity conflicts remain separate with a review item; this is a fixed lexical rule, not fuzzy matching. A merged physical branch retains every discovery observation and validation-attempt provenance but receives one validation-coverage credit. D50/D51 require every exact observation to occur exactly once across retained and excluded physical outputs, exclude a coordinate-derived branch outside the frozen pilot geography, retain an inside-set searched/derived mismatch with review, and rebuild brands/evidence/offerings/diagnostics from retained branches only. D52 independently approved the private method-pilot run `competitors_pilot_1e93cdf516f542c6`: 64 retained branches, 33 brands, 2 excluded, 27 confirmed, 28 possible, 9 unresolved, 51 resolved geocodes, 1 ambiguous, 12 missing-postal, 94/94 exact observations represented once, and all 17 gates/manifest/secret checks passed. These are pilot-method counts only, not Singapore-wide measures. National design now follows `national-competitor-design.md`: freeze a non-competitor candidate-node set before any competitor inspection, apply complete identical catchment protocol to that set, and run a stratified outside-candidate audit. No national acquisition, ranking or coverage claim is authorised until the document's pending user decisions are versioned and approved.

For every shortlisted catchment, validate address/unit, operating status, mathematics provision, overlapping P1–S4 levels and source date. `confirmed` requires a current operator locations/centre page and branch-specific subject/level or timetable evidence. Generic brand descriptions, directory categories and map pins remain `possible`. Seek an independent current signal for operating status, preserve contradictions/relocations, and record geocode candidates, match quality and adjudication. Recheck 100% of shortlisted records and every possible branch capable of changing a Pareto result. Before claiming national coverage, freeze an audit sample after the initial inventory size is known and report discovery-channel yield/overlap. No outreach is authorised in this pass.

Publish confirmed in-scope branch counts and possible additional branch counts. The frozen primary competition measure is confirmed in-scope branch count within the catchment; the uncertainty upper bound is confirmed plus every possible in-scope branch. No fractional distance or level-overlap weights enter the primary measure. Show distance bands and P1–S4 level overlap descriptively. A complex decay/overlap measure may be a separately labelled secondary sensitivity, but it cannot replace the primary measure after results are inspected. Retain branches per 1,000 target residents as a descriptive diagnostic only. Test duplicate/closed/planned branches, boundary effects and unobserved coverage. A branch is not equivalent to seats, market share or success; existing placement is revealed location behaviour only and unavailable capacity remains unknown. Count other education-provider branches separately and keep optional cluster effects out of the competition count.

## 6. Optional later premises due diligence

For each candidate premises, maintain a checklist with pass/fail/unknown/not-applicable outcomes, evidence, authority, review dates and expiry. S012 establishes that a tuition centre is a commercial-school use and identifies property-type-specific planning/consent routes. S013 supplies the public lodgment conditions. S014 supports manual allowable-use screening for shophouses/selected properties but is not approved for bulk capture. S015 establishes the SCDF/QP/approval/FSC questions. Record listing availability independently from permission.

Keep three compliance layers distinct: (1) premises use and fire safety under URA/SCDF, (2) school/operator registration and applicability under the Education Act/MOE process, and (3) ordinary commercial and lease due diligence. S024 records the current Education Act definition of a school and section 21 registration rule; S025 records ACRA guidance that business activities operating academic schools, learning centres or training centres may be referred to MOE during name reservation. These pages identify checks, not a conclusion about any future operator. Determine applicability from current facts and authoritative advice. This methodology is not legal advice.

Assign exactly one status:

- `screened_feasible`: a current identifiable unit has been reviewed against the applicable URA/SCDF public rules; no known mandatory public blocker is observed; every missing owner, URA and SCDF/QP confirmation is explicit.
- `verified_eligible`: all of the above plus current unit-specific authoritative evidence for every mandatory **premises** requirement—owner/landlord consent; approved use, URA lodgment acknowledgment or planning decision as applicable; and QP determination plus SCDF approval/exemption/FSC evidence as applicable. This status does not certify school/operator registration or ordinary commercial/lease due diligence.
- `ineligible`: at least one mandatory requirement has a confirmed failure.
- `unresolved`: evidence is insufficient, inaccessible or contradictory.

A listing/agent statement of “approved” is a claim, not authoritative evidence. An unavailable check is not a pass.

If optional unit due diligence is undertaken, a `screened_feasible` premises with an evidenced cost scenario may strengthen a conditional commercial-node recommendation, provided every remaining confirmation is explicit; its absence does not block the node recommendation. It is not lease-ready. A lease-ready recommendation requires current availability, quoted/all-in cost, `verified_eligible` premises evidence, applicable school/operator registration checks and ordinary commercial/lease due diligence. Premises that are `ineligible` leave the unit comparison pool; `unresolved` premises remain investigation leads. Reconfirm all cited guidance and unit evidence before any lease decision; this method does not replace advice from URA, SCDF, MOE, the owner, a Qualified Person or professional advisers.

S017 and S018 remain rejected for systematic acquisition under their terms, S019 remains unconfirmed, and S016 is restricted manual context whose observations may not be reproduced or stored in the public portfolio. Do not map S016 streets to nodes or derive published node rent ranges from it. Until a licensed/consented unit inventory supplies current availability, quoted rent and all-in charge fields, no current-unit comparison or lease-ready recommendation may be constructed. This limitation does not prevent node-level cost comparison under a generic `operator_assumption` rent grid.

## 7. Model operating break-even and inverse affordability

All monetary inputs use SGD; recurring inputs are per month unless an explicit conversion is stored. Baselines remain unset. Define `N` as active unique students purchasing one recurring mathematics place each. If multiple products or levels have different economics, later model segments explicitly and reconcile segment enrolments to unique pupils; do not silently mix seats, lessons and students.

| Variable | Meaning | Evidence/review requirement |
|---|---|---|
| `p` | Effective monthly recurring fee per student, after discounts/refunds | Fee schedules and a documented collection/discount policy |
| `v` | Variable cost per student-month, excluding separately modelled class costs | Materials, transaction charges and relevant variable services |
| `O` | Monthly non-occupancy fixed operating costs | Baseline payroll, utilities, insurance, software, administration, owner compensation and recurring marketing as applicable; exclude occupancy and separately modelled class costs |
| `R` | Monthly occupancy-cost assumption | Generic rent/charges assumption for node scenarios; later unit quote only with licensed/consented evidence |
| `C` | Feasible active-student capacity | Rooms, timetable, class size, levels, teacher availability and a consistent enrolment unit |
| `b`, `k` | Students per additional class and monthly cost per class, when relevant | Timetable/staffing evidence; no default class size or wage |
| `accessible_target_population_proxy` | Resident target-age population allocated to the node catchment | Directly measured proxy field; not demand, customers or an enrolment forecast |
| `effective_capture_share` | Optional combined participation-and-capture fraction | One illustrative combined assumption only; participation and operator capture are not separately identified by available evidence |

S020 demonstrates that branch-, level-, lesson-count- and period-specific public fee schedules exist, but such a comparator is not an operating baseline. Convert a block charge to a monthly or per-lesson quantity only from its observed dates/lesson count and retain both original and converted values. S021 is only `accessible_unconfirmed`: its detailed HES table could not be directly inspected. Do not use a value from it until verified; even then, household tuition expenditure would not be a participation rate, mathematics fee, local willingness-to-pay estimate or demand conversion. If an illustrative demand conversion is later requested, use one bounded `effective_capture_share`; do not claim the data separately identify participation and operator capture.

For conditional node analysis, use a versioned generic `operator_assumption` grid of monthly occupancy costs, including an adverse value. These values are not observed market rents and must be applied consistently across nodes within a scenario. S016 is restricted manual context only and is not a publishable analytical input. A later licensed node-specific rent source may replace the generic grid through a versioned contract extension. Unit-level analysis must use a current quoted/all-in cost scenario and separately evaluate service charges, GST, deposits and permission.

For the simple linear case, contribution per student is `m = p - v`, total fixed cost is `F = O + R`, and monthly operating surplus is `N × m - F`. If `m > 0`, `break_even_active_enrolments = ceil(F / m)` and break-even utilisation is that value divided by positive `C`. Values above 1 mean capacity cannot support break-even. If `m <= 0` and `F > 0`, no finite break-even exists. Handle zero fixed costs directly; do not emit invalid infinity/NaN values or imply viability with no contribution.

Calculate `required_capture_share = break_even_active_enrolments / accessible_target_population_proxy`. Do not clamp values above 1. Return null with an explicit reason if either input is null, or if the population proxy is zero; a zero denominator is undefined, including when break-even is zero. Report capacity infeasibility separately. This is an interpretive burden measure derived from the demand dimension and therefore is not weighted again in the financial dimension.

For a stated target enrolment `N <= C`, calculate `maximum_affordable_monthly_occupancy_cost = N × (p - v) - O` in the linear model. In a stepped model also subtract the applicable class cost, for example `k × ceil(N / b)`. If the raw value is negative, report zero plus a `no_nonnegative_occupancy_cost_affordable` flag; if inputs are missing or `N > C`, return null with a reason. Use a common target enrolment within a comparison scenario unless node-specific operating evidence supports otherwise; do not derive it from the population proxy and count the same advantage twice. Report this inverse break-even output alongside break-even active enrolments, utilisation and required capture share.

Class-based tutor costs are usually discontinuous: for a justified single-segment illustration, surplus is `N × (p - v) - O - R - k × ceil(N / b)`. Where this model is adopted, keep `k` out of `O`, `R` and `v`. Search feasible integer enrolments within `C` for the first nonnegative surplus, and inspect the entire range because opening another class can reduce surplus again. A real multi-level timetable must use its feasible class allocation rather than assume all students can share a class. Store the model type and return `capacity_infeasible` if no feasible enrolment works. Report staffing-cost alternatives instead of relying on the simple formula when its assumptions fail.

Capacity must be reconstructed from actual timetable assumptions: teaching spaces × usable lesson slots × seats per class, divided by required slots per student, with level mix and staffing constraints. Seat-slots and unique students are not interchangeable. The planning baseline is one recurring place per student; additional weekly sessions reduce available enrolment capacity unless supported by additional slots.

The four core financial outputs are `break_even_active_enrolments`, break-even utilisation, `required_capture_share` and `maximum_affordable_monthly_occupancy_cost`. Opening-cash, working-capital and payback modelling is outside this core release and would require a later versioned extension with explicit timing, deposit, refund, tax, financing and owner-pay assumptions.

Expansion cannibalisation is an optional conditional analysis, not a core hypothesis. When authorised existing-network evidence is available, measure net incremental network cash flow, including cannibalised contribution and shared-cost changes. Otherwise report stand-alone results without making the general shortlist depend on private operator data.

## 8. Compare alternatives and expose uncertainty

Apply data-quality and minimum-feasibility gates first. For each complete operating scenario, build a node Pareto frontier on exactly four dimensions: demand, accessibility, competition and financial viability. Demand uses `accessible_target_population_proxy`; competition uses confirmed in-scope catchment branch count, with confirmed-plus-all-possible as an uncertainty case. Financial viability may use multiple outputs, but combine their direction-aligned percentile ranks inside one finance dimension. Do not use `required_capture_share` as a weighted finance input because it divides by the population proxy. Generic rent values and other common financial assumptions produce financial ties and may not be used to manufacture node differences; only supported node-specific inputs can differentiate nodes financially. A premises record is not required. Preserve the generic rent grid and sensitivity with every conditional node result. Compare optional unit alternatives separately and preserve their four statuses.

Within every scenario, rank admissible non-dominated nodes under exactly five demand/accessibility/competition/finance weight profiles: balanced `25/25/25/25`; demand `40/20/20/20`; accessibility `20/40/20/20`; competition `20/20/40/20`; and finance `20/20/20/40`. Across every scenario × profile combination, select up to three nodes by highest top-three frequency. Break ties by lower median rank, then lower worst-case rank, then stable node ID. Publish counts, denominators, frequencies and full rank distributions for all admissible candidates. Do not force three and do not label the result objectively best.

Planned tests include age-band allocation and cohort shifts; geography/boundary vintages; area versus residential weighting; catchment modes, thresholds and anchors; competitor omissions/duplicates/status and the possible-inclusive upper bound; optional secondary distance/level-overlap formulations; fees/discounts, generic rent assumptions, variable costs, class costs, capacity and utilisation; ramp-up/churn and the optional combined effective-capture assumption; and fit-out/deposits. Cluster effects and expansion cannibalisation are optional extensions. Start with one-way sensitivities and a coherent, explicit plausible scenario grid. Avoid impossible combinations and independently sampling related parameters without justification.

Use interval bounds where probability distributions lack evidence. Monte Carlo is optional only after defensible distributions/dependencies are recorded, with seed and draws for reproducibility. Report feasibility changes, break-even ranges, Pareto-membership frequency, top-three frequency and rank distributions over the complete versioned scenario/profile grid. These are robustness descriptions, not probabilities or threshold tests; there is no 70% pass/fail rule. Also report the former hypothesis as a descriptive contrast between a population-only ordering and the full decision procedure. Hold out final recommendation selection until these checks are complete.
